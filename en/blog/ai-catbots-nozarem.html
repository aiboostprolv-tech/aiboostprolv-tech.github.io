AI chatbot for business: 16 industries where it helps

30.10.2026 · 10 min read · Kristīne Vasiļjeva

The question I hear most often is: "Would a chatbot work for my business too?" The answer almost always comes down to one thing: do your customers keep asking the same questions? If they do, a chatbot saves you time and answers the customer straight away — in the evening and at weekends too.

Below are sixteen industries, with the real questions customers ask in each and what a chatbot can do about them. At the end — an honest look at when you don't need one.

1. Restaurants and cafés

Customers ask: "Are you open late tonight?", "Do you have vegetarian dishes?", "Can I book a table for 8?", "Do you host banquets?"

A chatbot can: give your opening hours, answer menu questions, collect a table or banquet request and send it to you.

A chatbot should only answer allergen questions from your own menu information. If that information isn't there, it should suggest contacting the restaurant rather than guess.

2. Beauty salons, hairdressers and spas

Customers ask: "How much is colouring for long hair?", "How long does a manicure take?", "Any free slots on Saturday?", "How do I cancel my appointment?"

A chatbot can: quote prices from your price list, explain treatments, collect booking requests and remind people of your cancellation policy.

3. Online shops

Customers ask: "How much is delivery to a parcel locker?", "How do I return an item?", "Which size will fit me?", "When will my order arrive?"

A chatbot can: answer questions about delivery, returns and sizing at any hour. Checking an order's status needs a connection to your shop system — that's an integration.

4. Guesthouses, country stays and campsites

Customers ask: "What time is check-in?", "Can I bring my dog?", "Is breakfast included?", "How do I get to you?"

A chatbot can: answer foreign guests in English and Russian while you're asleep or welcoming other guests, and collect enquiries for specific dates.

5. Car services and tyre fitting

Customers ask: "How much is a tyre change for R16?", "Do I need to book in advance?", "Do you store tyres?", "How long does a service take?"

A chatbot can: in peak season, when the phone never stops, answer the standard questions and collect requests — you only call back the people who really need it.

6. Dentists, clinics and health services

Customers ask: "Are you taking new patients?", "How much is a consultation?", "Do you work with insurance?", "Where can I park?"

A chatbot can: answer practical questions and collect appointment requests.

Important: a chatbot must not give diagnoses or treatment advice. It always passes medical questions to a specialist.

7. Real estate and construction

Customers ask: "Is the flat still available?", "Can I view it this week?", "How much does a roof replacement cost?", "Do you work outside Riga?"

A chatbot can: find out the basics — budget, area, timeline — and hand you a request that's already prepared. You spend your time talking to serious clients.

8. Courses, coaches and language schools

Customers ask: "When does the next group start?", "Which level is right for me?", "Is there a trial lesson?", "Can I pay in instalments?"

A chatbot can: explain your schedule and prices, help people choose a group and sign them up for a trial lesson.

9. Senior care and social services

Families ask: "How much is 24-hour care?", "Which documents are needed?", "Can we come and have a look?", "Is there municipal co-funding?"

A chatbot can: answer in the evening, when the family finally sits down to look for information, and pass sensitive questions to a person.

10. Sports clubs, gyms and yoga studios

Customers ask: "How much is a monthly membership?", "Is the first visit free?", "When are the evening classes?", "Can I freeze my membership?"

A chatbot can: explain memberships and the timetable, answer questions about the rules and sign people up for a first class.

11. Accountants, lawyers and consultants

Customers ask: "How much is bookkeeping for a company with 5 employees?", "Do you help with the annual return?", "Can the consultation be online?", "Which documents should I prepare?"

A chatbot can: explain your services and how pricing works, collect details of the client's situation and book a consultation.

A chatbot doesn't give legal or tax advice on a specific situation — that stays with the specialist.

12. Vet clinics and pet services

Customers ask: "Can you see us today?", "How much is a vaccination?", "Do you have a dog groomer?", "Can you look after my cat for a week?"

A chatbot can: answer questions about services and prices, collect appointment requests and, in urgent cases, give the phone number straight away.

13. Tourism, tours and events

Customers ask: "Is the tour in English?", "How many people can be in a group?", "Can you organise a birthday party?", "What's included in the price?"

A chatbot can: answer in three languages, explain the programme and what the price includes, and collect a request for a specific date and group size.

14. Car rental, transport and couriers

Customers ask: "How much is a van for a day?", "Do I need a deposit?", "Do you move furniture to Liepāja?", "How quickly can you come?"

A chatbot can: explain terms and prices, find out the route, date and size of the load, and pass the request on to you.

15. Cleaning, repairs and home services

Customers ask: "How much is a clean after renovation?", "Do you work at weekends?", "Can you replace the plumbing?", "Do you come out to Jūrmala?"

A chatbot can: find out the scope of work, address and preferred time, quote prices where they are fixed, and prepare a request for a quote.

16. Shops, manufacturers and small producers

Customers ask: "Is this in stock?", "Do you sell wholesale?", "Where can I buy your products?", "Do you ship abroad?"

A chatbot can: answer questions about your range, where to buy and shipping, and collect wholesale enquiries.

Is your industry not on the list? The principle is the same: if customers keep asking the same questions, a chatbot can help.

What every industry has in common

  • Repeat questions. If you answer the same questions every day, a chatbot can do it for you.
  • Questions outside working hours. A customer who gets an answer straight away is less likely to go to a competitor.
  • Several languages. The chatbot answers in Latvian, Russian and English — in the customer's language.
  • Answers only from your content. The chatbot works from your website, price list and policies — it doesn't make things up.

When you don't need a chatbot

Honestly — not always. A chatbot adds little if:

  • you only have a few customers a month and talk to each of them personally;
  • every request is unique and needs a long individual conversation;
  • your customers hardly visit your website and just phone you.

In that case you're better off investing in a well-kept Google Business Profile or a clear website.

How to get started

  1. Write down the 10 questions customers ask you most often.
  2. Next to each one, write the answer you'd give yourself.
  3. Mark the cases where a person needs to take over the conversation.

Frequently asked questions

Is an AI chatbot worth it for a small business?

Yes, if customers often ask the same questions — about prices, opening hours, bookings or delivery. If every customer is a completely different case, the benefit is smaller.

How much does an AI chatbot cost?

An AI chatbot from AiBoost Pro costs from €150 one-off, excluding VAT. The price depends on the number of languages, integrations and how complex the scenarios are. It is usually ready in 1–2 days.

Can the chatbot book appointments?

The chatbot can collect a request — name, contact details and preferred time — and pass it on to you. Connecting it directly to a booking system is an integration, and that affects the price.

Which languages does the chatbot speak?

Latvian, Russian and English. It replies in the language the customer writes in.

What does the chatbot do when it doesn't know the answer?

A well-built chatbot doesn't make up an answer. It says it doesn't know and offers to put the customer in touch with a person — by phone, WhatsApp or email.
